Of *course* we are shaped by the choices presented to us!

At the beginning of August, Lauren Leek published [Temperature Zero for Culture:
Why Everything Is Starting to Look the
Same](https://laurenleek.substack.com/p/temperature-zero-for-culture-why).
Optimizing algorithms (especially those without forced exploration) smooth away
variance and regress to means and modes.

This is Cory Doctorow's "late stage capitalism"---all the businesses look the
same (because they are). It's [shaping language]({% link
_posts/2026-05-07-machines-humans.md %}), driving a collapse in [the stories we
tell]({% link _posts/2026-09-20-turkle.md %}#reclaiming-conversation). It's the
predictable result of human--choice combinations, where the "choice" side is
algorithmically optimized.

These tools---demographic analysis for launching new franchises, music and show
recommendations, ad placement optimization---are *not just tools*.

Remember, ["the limits of my language mean the limits of my world"]({% link
_writings/bsms-statement.md %}), and it's not *just* LLMs that do this.
Practically every optimizing system we've built in the last several decades
wants to smooth out variance.

I don't want to be smoothed out.

Fortunately Leek argues we can make a policy choice to fix this: forced
exploration helps the [multi-armed bandit
problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-armed_bandit), although it's not
clear to me how to legislate such forced alternatives. Still, breaking up
monopolies would help!
